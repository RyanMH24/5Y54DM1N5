## The ActiveDirectory module

PowerShell's `ActiveDirectory` module adds cmdlets for working directly
with [AD](/glossary/ad) — the same directory service covered in Identity &
Access Fundamentals, now from the command line instead of a GUI console.
These cmdlets follow the same `Verb-Noun` pattern as everything else in
PowerShell, so `Get-ADUser`, `New-ADUser`, and `Disable-ADUser` all do
roughly what their names suggest.

## Finding and inspecting users

`Get-ADUser -Filter *` lists every user in the directory; narrowing with
`-Filter "Name -like 'j*'"` finds just the ones whose name starts with
"j". Piping the result into `Select-Object` picks out specific
properties: `Get-ADUser -Identity jsmith -Properties * | Select-Object
Name, LastLogonDate` shows exactly the fields you need instead of a wall of
output.

## Creating and disabling accounts

`New-ADUser -Name "Jane Smith" -SamAccountName jsmith -Enabled $true`
provisions a new account — the scripted version of the onboarding step
covered in Identity & Access Fundamentals. Offboarding works the same way
in reverse: `Disable-ADUser -Identity jsmith` immediately blocks that
account from authenticating anywhere, without deleting it — useful when a
departing employee's access needs to be cut off right away, but their
account and its history might still need to be reviewed first.

## Why this matters day to day

Doing this by hand in a GUI console doesn't scale past a handful of
accounts. A script built around `Get-ADUser`, `New-ADUser`, and
`Disable-ADUser` turns a batch onboarding of twenty new hires, or an
emergency mass-disable during an incident, into something that runs in
seconds instead of taking an afternoon of repetitive clicking.
