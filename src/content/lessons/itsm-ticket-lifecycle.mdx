## Intake: where tickets come from

A ticket usually doesn't start with an agent — it starts with a user
submitting a request, or a monitoring system firing an alert automatically.
Either way, it lands in the [ticket queue](/glossary/ticket-queue): the
ordered backlog of open issues waiting to be worked. Intake itself isn't
really a decision an agent makes; it's just the ticket showing up. The
real work starts with the next step.

## Triage: assessing what you've got

**Triage** is the first thing a human does with a new ticket: read it,
figure out roughly how urgent and how complex it is, and categorize it so
it gets routed and prioritized correctly. A "my monitor won't turn on"
ticket and a "the whole floor lost network access" ticket both arrive the
same way, but they shouldn't be worked the same way — triage is where that
distinction gets made, before any actual troubleshooting begins.

## Escalation: knowing when it's not yours to fix

Not every ticket should stay with the first person who picks it up.
[Escalation](/glossary/escalation) means handing a ticket to a more senior
or specialized team once it's clear the current tier can't resolve it —
not as a last resort after struggling for hours, but as soon as that
becomes clear. A good escalation includes what's already been tried, so the
next team isn't starting from zero. Escalating too late wastes the user's
time; escalating a ticket you could've solved yourself wastes the senior
team's time. Getting that judgment right is most of what separates a
strong support agent from a mediocre one.

## Closing: finishing the job properly

Once the underlying problem is actually fixed, a ticket gets closed — but
closing isn't just clicking a button. Recording the **resolution** (what
was actually done) matters: the next person who hits a similar issue,
possibly on a completely different ticket months later, benefits from being
able to search for how this one was solved. An [SLA](/glossary/sla) often
measures time-to-close, but a fast close with no useful resolution notes is
a worse outcome than a slightly slower one that leaves a real trail.

## Practice it

Reading about the lifecycle only gets you so far — walk a real ticket
through triage, escalation, and closing yourself in the
